Introduction - Alister McLaughlin.


In the Introduction to this month's newsletter, we speak with Alister McLaughlin - Director & Board Member.


Hi team,

One of the things that makes me proud of TDX is seeing our people take opportunities to learn, step up and look after one another. This edition brings together some great examples from across TDX and LDX.

Our relationships with our suppliers and OEM partners have always been an important part of the business. The recent Volvo training trip to Sweden and customer visit to Yanmar in Japan show the value of those relationships. Getting alongside the people who design and build our equipment gives our teams and customers a deeper understanding of the machines, and strengthens the connections behind them.

Closer to home, our Tauranga team have moved into their new branch which is going to be a real asset to Tauranga and the wider Central North Island region. The new Gisborne branch is taking shape. From modest beginnings, the team has built strong customer relationships through hard work and dependable support, including during some very challenging times for the region. It's pleasing to see that commitment backed by better facilities and room to grow.


You'll also read about Charlie making his mark at LDX and our NRL trip winners enjoying well-earned recognition, with thanks to Donaldson for their support. These stories remind us how much individuals contribute by taking ownership, supporting their teammates and living our values.

Brodie and Scott's reflections on Take Care are a timely reminder that looking after ourselves and each other starts with the decisions we make every day. Getting home safely to our families matters above everything else.

Thank you for the work you do across the business. Your commitment to our customers and one another is what keeps TDX and LDX moving forward while keeping our customers going.

Alister

Sales Boost in Sweden: A hands-on Volvo experience


A group of TDX representatives recently traveled to Sweden for a Volvo Sales Boost trip, here is an account of their experience as told by Auckland Sales Manager, Nigel Davies.


For six of us from TDX, the recent Volvo Oceania Sales Boost trip to Eskilstuna, Sweden, was an excellent opportunity to build our product knowledge, strengthen our sales capability and experience some exciting new equipment firsthand.

Alongside Stephane Bessette, Dan Clavelle, Andre Muller, Trevor Newport and Chris Schreuder, I joined 34 representatives from CJD, the Australian Volvo dealer, as well as Jean and Mark from New Caledonia, for five days of training and learning.

One of the biggest highlights was getting hands-on with some of Volvo's latest machines at the Volvo Customer Center. We had the opportunity to experience the A30 Electric, the new L90 Electric and the impressive new L260HD.

The L260HD is particularly exciting for us, with its 8.2m³ bucket giving TDX a strong answer to the CAT 982. Being able to see and experience these machines in person makes a real difference when it comes to understanding their capabilities and communicating their benefits to customers.


Another standout was visiting Volvo's articulated hauler factory at Braås. Seeing the operation behind these machines and understanding more about how they are built was a fantastic experience.

The trip wasn't just about the iron, either. We also took part in classroom sessions covering ActiveCare, uptime services and Connected Map, giving us a broader understanding of Volvo's support offering and how we can deliver greater value to customers beyond the machine itself.

A key theme throughout the week was value-based selling. Rather than focusing solely on specifications and price, we explored how to better understand customer needs and demonstrate the value Volvo equipment and services can deliver. On the final day, we put that learning into practice with team presentations, bringing everything together.

Overall, the trip was excellent and certainly lived up to its Sales Boost name. We returned with greater confidence, stronger product knowledge and fresh ideas to take into the market.

A big thanks to TDX, Andrew Falloon and Joe Gradwell for supporting the event and making it possible for such a large part of our sales team to attend. It was a fantastic investment in our people and our customers.
 

Japan trip delivers a closer look at Yanmar


TDX customers enjoy a behind-the-scenes look at Yanmar manufacturing, alongside a taste of Japanese culture and hospitality.


A trip to Japan gave nine TDX customers an unforgettable opportunity to get closer to the Yanmar brand, see its manufacturing operations firsthand and experience some of the country's rich history and culture.

Led by Richard Papps, TDX National Sales Manager - Yanmar, the group spent a week travelling between Nagasaki, Kyoto and Osaka, visiting key Yanmar facilities and enjoying a packed programme of sightseeing and cultural experiences.

The journey began in Nagasaki, where the group visited the Nagasaki Peace Park and Atomic Bomb Museum. The experience offered an eye-opening perspective on one of the most significant events in modern history, before the group explored local gardens and temples and took a boat trip to Gunkanjima, a former coal-mining island off the coast.

The focus then shifted to Yanmar's operations, with a visit to its Construction factory providing a particular highlight for customers.

Seeing how the machines are built gave the group a new appreciation for the manufacturing process, with one of the biggest surprises being the factory's production system. Rather than building machines in batches of the same model, Yanmar moves between different models along the production line, with a machine completed approximately every nine minutes.

The visit also gave customers the opportunity to view and provide feedback on the new SV10, a model TDX is investigating bringing to New Zealand.

“The customers absolutely loved seeing how the machines are built and learning more about the process behind them,” says Richard. “They were really surprised by the way the production line operates and how quickly each machine comes off the line.”

The group also travelled by bullet train to Kyoto, where they split into two teams to visit the Yanmar Engine Factory and Yanmar Group Museum, before swapping locations to ensure everyone experienced both.

Both visits proved popular, offering further insight into Yanmar's engineering expertise, history and broader business operations.

Of course, the trip was about more than machinery. The group enjoyed a riverboat cruise along the Hozu River, an authentic traditional Kyoto lunch and a visit to Kyoto's famous Golden Temple.

The final stop was Osaka, where the group viewed Yanmar Holdings' impressive high-rise building near Osaka Station before enjoying some free time to explore the city.

Richard says the week was a great opportunity for customers to strengthen their connection with Yanmar while experiencing a different culture.

“Everyone had a fantastic time. It was a great mix of learning about the brand, seeing the manufacturing process and experiencing Japanese culture, food and hospitality.”

A big thank you goes to Yanmar, particularly Inoue-san, for hosting the group and organising some memorable experiences and outstanding meals throughout the trip.

Richard also acknowledged Sarah King for the considerable behind-the-scenes work involved in coordinating flights and putting the itinerary together.

After an incredible week, the group began the long journey home on 17 October, bringing back plenty of new knowledge, great memories and an even stronger appreciation for Yanmar.

Gisborne branch set for a big step forward


TDX's new Gisborne branch is taking shape, with a bigger workshop, dedicated yard space and improved facilities set to strengthen customer support across the region.


TDX is nearing the finish line on its new Gisborne branch, with the team preparing to move into a purpose-built location that will provide more room to grow and an even better level of service for customers.

The site is now entering the final stages of cladding and fit-out, with completion expected in late November. The team will begin relocating in January, with the new branch expected to become operational shortly afterwards.

For National Aftersales Manager Craig Wymer, the move represents an important milestone for TDX in a region where the business has steadily established its presence over the past seven years.

“We started out small in Gisborne, operating from a portacom on a shared site. This new location is the next step for us, giving TDX a more professional, standalone presence and the facilities to better support our customers,” says Craig.

The Gisborne team currently comprises two mechanics and one parts correspondent, all of whom have been with TDX for several years. Their commitment to customers has helped build a strong reputation in an expansive geographical region that has faced its fair share of challenges in recent years.

“Gisborne has experienced some significant weather events, and our customers and communities have had a tough few years. As an organisation, TDX has stepped up to support locals through those difficult times, and our team has done a fantastic job of looking after customers when they need us most,” Craig says.

The new premises will make a tangible difference to the way the branch operates. The workshop will increase in size by approximately 40 per cent, while the additional yard space will provide much-needed room for equipment storage, machine movements and day-to-day operations.

Just as importantly, the site gives TDX its own dedicated footprint, creating opportunities to bring in and display TDX-supported machines for customers to view. The ability to hold stock machines on-site will also improve the branch's sales presence and visibility.

“Our current premises are quite constrained, so having our own yard and workshop space will make a real difference. We'll have more room to work efficiently, better facilities for our mechanics and the opportunity to showcase the machines we support,” Craig says.

While the new building and facilities are a significant improvement, the longer-term goal is to continue growing the Gisborne operation, both in team size and turnover.



“TDX has a loyal customer base in Gisborne, and we want to keep building on those relationships. Ultimately, it's about improving our service offering, growing the branch and making sure we're well positioned to support our customers into the future,” says Craig.

With the new site almost ready, Gisborne is looking forward to an exciting new chapter for TDX.

Hero Staff Member - Charlie Farr


Charlie makes his mark at LDX


Just over 12-months after joining TDX, Charlie is taking the next step in his career, moving from the Tauranga branch to LDX in Cambridge, where he is already making a positive impression.

Charlie successfully applied for the newly created role following sustained growth at LDX. Now approaching two months in the position, he has quickly settled into the team and is proving to be exactly the right person for the job.

LDX manager Leon Proctor says finding a new team member who could step into the role and make an immediate contribution was important.

“With the continued growth at LDX, we needed someone who could hit the ground running, and Charlie is proving to be that person,” Leon says.

Charlie has recently taken on responsibility for coordinating planned services across the Service team, including everything from job creation to ensuring parts are available when they are needed. He has embraced the challenge from day one, taking ownership and showing a real willingness to step up and support the wider team.

“Charlie is taking ownership of coordinating parts and making sure the supply is there for our services and repairs. We treat our workshop as our biggest customer, and our goal is to provide a 'Rolls Royce' level of service. Charlie has quickly embraced that responsibility and is doing an outstanding job of supporting the team and keeping things running smoothly,” Leon says.

“It's about making sure the team has what it needs, when it needs it, and Charlie is doing a great job of taking responsibility for that.”

The role also comes with a significant level of trust, particularly as Leon is based in a different city and relies on Charlie to manage his responsibilities independently.



“He's a young guy, but he has a wise head on his shoulders. He's been given an opportunity to step up into a role that requires a lot of trust, and he's handling that really well,” Leon says.

While Charlie is still relatively early in his LDX journey, Leon believes the foundations are in place for a successful future.

“There's every chance Charlie can have a long and prosperous future here. The early indications are that he has huge potential, and I'm looking forward to seeing him continue to develop in his role and make an even bigger contribution to LDX.”
