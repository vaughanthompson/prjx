Garry Woods - General Manager LDX


In the Introduction to this month's newsletter, we speak with Garry Woods - General Manager LDX.


The past few weeks have certainly been a hive of activity across both TDX and LDX, and there is plenty to share with you in this month's newsletter.

One of the highlights has been the NRL Trip of a Lifetime campaign, proudly supported by our good friends at Donaldson. The campaign culminated in four very worthy winners earning a trip to Sydney for the NRL Grand Final and a factory tour. The real success of the campaign was the fantastic level of engagement right across the business.

The number of nominations was large, with staff recognising colleagues who regularly go above and beyond in demonstrating the Group's Values. It was a great reminder that there are many unsung heroes across TDX and LDX doing good work every day. The ELT had a tough job in voting for the nominations, and I think we should all be justifiably proud of the way everyone got behind the campaign. Congratulations to everyone who was nominated.

Importantly, the campaign also brought us back to what really makes our business tick: our Values and focus on our Customers. These are more than words; they are the Values that guide how we operate every day.

There has also been plenty happening behind the scenes with our Business Strategy. The ELT has spent the past six months developing a three-year strategic roadmap covering 2027-2030. This work culminated recently in a two-day workshop with Senior Management, and we're now moving into the next phase, with working groups established to further develop our prioritised operational strategies to turn our goals and objectives into action.

From a business perspective, the end of September marks the halfway point of the financial year. It's hard to believe how quickly the first six months have gone. Despite some challenges, we're tracking well and are in the ballpark of achieving our EBIT objectives - but we know we need to lift our performance in the second half to get there.

Last financial year, we reached our budget EBIT objectives in the final month, so we know the importance of keeping focused right through to year end.

There are a number of projects and initiatives underway that will help simplify the way we work and move both TDX and LDX forward. These include the appointment of our new Health and Safety Manager and HR Manager, Uptime enhancements and the new TDX Tauranga branch. You can read more about them in this edition.

Finally you would have received your invitation to participate in the Safety culture survey. We encourage everyone to invest a little bit of time into what is a very important voice that each of you have to give us your honest feedback and insights into our ongoing safety journey.

From the Board and ELT, thanks again for your continued support, commitment and hard work. And, as always, please keep your, and others Safety front of mind at all times.

 

What does 'Take Care' mean to you?


This month, we asked John O'Callaghan and Joe Gradwell to share what Take Care means to them.


John O'Callaghan:

For me, Take Care means ensuring that every decision balances financial performance with the wellbeing of our people, the protection of our environment, and the effective management of risk.

It requires creating a culture where employees are empowered to identify hazards, raise concerns, and take action, while ensuring adequate investment in safety, compliance, and sustainable business practices.

Ultimately, it reflects a leadership commitment that no commercial outcome is more important than keeping our people safe and protecting the long-term future of TDX.

Joe Gradwell:

What does "Take Care" mean to me?

To me, it means putting our people first. It means looking out for each other every day and living our commitment to See Something. Say Something. Do Something.

Having spent many years on the tools myself, I know how easy it is to walk past something or think, "She'll be right, it'll only take a minute." We've all done it at some stage. But it's that one time things don't go to plan that changes a life forever.


One of the biggest signs of a strong safety culture is doing the right thing when nobody is watching. Not because someone told you to, not because you're being audited, and not because your manager is standing beside you, but because you genuinely care about your own safety and the safety of the people around you.

Nobody comes to work expecting to get hurt. It's usually the routine jobs, the shortcuts, or the hazards we've seen a hundred times before that catch us out when we least expect it. That's why safety isn't about paperwork or ticking boxes, it's about having the courage to stop, think, and act before someone gets hurt.

Every time one of us identifies a hazard, speaks up, or fixes something that isn't right, we're not just preventing an incident, we're protecting a mate, a workmate, or someone else's family. The more people who are prepared to act, the stronger our safety culture becomes.

Sometimes it's worth asking yourself one simple question?

"What would my life, or someone else's life, look like if I made a split second decision that changed everything?"

Would you still be able to run around with your kids? Go fishing with your mates? Head into the hills for a hunt? Take the boat out for the weekend? Or simply enjoy the everyday moments with the people who matter most?

Those are the things we're protecting every single day.




To me, Take Care means we genuinely care enough to look out for one another. It means we hold each other accountable for the obvious hazards and the silly shortcuts that could have life changing consequences. Those conversations aren't about pointing fingers, they're about making sure everyone gets to go home at night.

At the end of the day, the best safety culture isn't built by policies, procedures or signs on the wall. It's built by people who consistently do the right thing, even when nobody is watching.

Because every time we see something, say something, and do something, we're building a culture where looking after each other is simply how we do business.

Nothing we do at TDX is so important that it's worth risking someone's future. We take care, because everyone deserves to go home safely.



 

Tauranga Team Gets Ready for a Bigger and Better Home!


Big changes are happening in Tauranga, with the TDX team moving into a brand-new home at 101 Hewletts Road from October 1.


After 12 years at the current site, the Tauranga branch has well and truly outgrown its surroundings. The new facility, just 1.0km away in a straight line, is three to four times the size of the existing branch and promises to give the team plenty more room to work, grow and support customers.

Located on the main road between Tauranga and Mount Maunganui, right next to the airport and not far from the port, it's also a much more prominent location for TDX.

Craig Wymer, TDX National Aftersales Manager - Tauranga, says the team is more than ready for the change.

“We've been crammed into the existing site for a long time, so having this much more space is going to make a huge difference. Everyone is feeling really energised about the move - it's a clean slate and a fresh start for the whole team.”

The new facility will deliver a bigger, better and safer workplace, as well as greater efficiencies and a better level of service for customers. It will also provide LDX with a stronger footprint and will allow TDX to bring some third-party logistics activity back inhouse.

“There's a lot of potential here,” says Craig.

“It gives us the room to expand and, importantly, gives us a much better opportunity to serve our customers. We can do things here that simply weren't possible in the old facility.”

With around 14 team members involved, moving an entire branch is no small job. But Craig says the challenge has actually helped bring the team together.

“The move itself is a massive logistical exercise, but everyone has really got behind it. We're working together and there's a real sense of excitement about what's ahead.”

The new site is currently being fitted out, with more work planned through October to get it fully up to TDX standard.

And there's another exciting opportunity down the track - prominent TDX signage at the new location.

“It's a great position for us,” says Craig.



“Being right alongside State Highway 2 gives us a chance to really lift the TDX presence in Tauranga. It feels like we're taking the branch to the next level.”

Uptime Team Roles, Responsibilities and Highlights


The TDX Uptime team is a small but integral part of the TDX ecosystem. Over recent months, the team has enjoyed a significant number of wins, both helping within the business as well as delivering an enhanced level of service to our customers. We had a chat with Laura to find out more about their responsibilities and highlights.


The TDX uptime team consists of four people and we work behind the scenes on helping keep machines running, supporting productivity and reducing disruption to our customers.

The team includes:

  • Ken - Warranty Administrator
  • Pierre - Warranty Manager
  • Kate - CSA Manager
  • Laura - Uptime Manager

Kate plays a key role in supporting our ASTMs and branches with CSAs, from setting them up through to the ongoing management of the contract. She helps make the process easier, keeps things moving and is always approachable and ready to help, with a smile. She has also helped improve the visibility and follow-up of our CSA portfolio, making it easier for the wider team to understand where contracts are at and where support is needed.

On the warranty side, Ken and Pierre bring a lot of knowledge and experience to the team, supporting our branches with cases and helping maximise the recovery available from our OEMs. They're always willing to share their knowledge and help branches work through a case, especially when things are not straightforward.

We work closely with Product Support, our branches and the wider TDX network across Warranty, CSAs and Connected Services. The three areas really complement each other and give us a much better picture of what is happening with a machine.

Warranty is there to support the customer when something goes wrong and help us maximise the support available from our OEMs. CSAs help us plan maintenance and manage costs over the life of the machine, while Connected Services gives us visibility of how the machine is actually being used and performing.

When you bring all of that information together, we can give branches and customers better advice, more time to plan the right response and potentially avoid unnecessary downtime.

Connected services are an increasingly important part of the business. Technology changes rapidly, especially amongst Volvo machinery, so a constant stream of data allows us to make informed decisions. It delivers huge benefits in terms of enhanced machine uptime.

TDX has achieved a very strong level of connectivity across the Volvo fleet in our region. That has also given us opportunities to work closely with Volvo, provide feedback and take part in trials as connected services continue to develop.

For us, the value isn't just in having access to digital tools. It's understanding the data, putting it into context and turning it into something useful for the branch or customer.

A real highlight of our job is making things easier and more manageable for our customers and branches. A lot of what we do happens behind the scenes, so we sometimes feel like an 'invisible force.'

As a team, we're always willing to go the extra mile, whether that means helping work through an issue, improving a process, providing better reporting or simply being there when someone needs support. The aim is to make things easier and give people the information they need to make better decisions.

I'm super proud of the Uptime team and their contribution to TDX and our customers.

Fulton Hogan re-signs significant order with TDX for Volvo and Yanmar machines


TDX recently received news that Fulton Hogan is committing to another significant machinery order - 45 machines with a value of over $18M. We spoke with Joe Gradwell to get some more information about the impressive deal.


Fulton Hogan have been a customer of TDX for many years, whilst we have an outstanding relationship with them you can never take anything for granted. Today more than ever before, there are many more competitors in the market, some of which engage in a price race to the bottom, in an effort to win business.

At TDX, we're incredibly fortunate to have built up a solid relationship with Fulton Hogan over the years. This is due in no small part to the efforts that Andrew Falloon puts in, to ensure that the customer receives the absolute best service and care possible.

It's acknowledged that TDX wasn't the cheapest, but what we offer in terms of our support and service - as well as products we represent helped secure the deal.

A further significance of this deal is that it helps provide an element of certainty as well as cashflow to the business, reassuring the Board and shareholders that we're not only heading in the right direction, but importantly have an order bank which will help lay a solid foundation for the next 3-4 year cycle with Fulton Hogan.

This greatly assists when it comes to decision making relating to planning and investment, especially when there are some elements of uneconomic uncertainty around the upcoming election and geopolitical happenings around the world. This deal is a great start and contributes to a level of protection going forward.

In addition to Andrew's efforts, I'd also like to recognise Richard Papps and the work he did from the Yanmar side.

Furthermore, I am extremely grateful to the wider TDX team for the support provided over the last few years in supporting the Fulton Hogan fleet, this all in turn contributes to customer peace of mind and satisfaction. This is across the board, from sales to aftersales, service, technicians in the field - it's an absolute team effort.

Results like this don't happen by chance, these are the sort of deals our competitors dream of winning, so thanks again to everyone involved who played a part in this success, we should all be extremely proud!


Now the hard work starts again, never take a win like this for granted and there's no room for complacency. We need to step up and give Fulton Hogan the confidence they have made the right decision.

We've done it in the past and I know we can do it again!

Hero Staff Member - Kenny Marshall


We have done things a little differently for this month's Hero a Staff Member. Rather than the 'traditional' feature, this story focuses on Safety as the Hero. With this in mind, we had a chat with new TDX Health and Safety Manager, Kenny Marshall.


Safety Starts With All of Us

Two months into his new role at TDX, Health and Safety Manager Kenny Marshall has already been getting out and about, visiting branches and meeting people across the business. What he's seen has reinforced something important: safety is not just a policy at TDX - it's part of how we work.

Originally from Scotland, Kenny has called New Zealand home for the past 21 years. A qualified Heavy Diesel Mechanic, he has worked across a range of industries and businesses, including Asplundh, Franklin Trees and Crown Equipment.

Along the way, health and safety became much more than just another part of his job, it became a passion.

"I was always interested in safety as a mechanic,” says Kenny.

"But I witnessed a very traumatic workplace injury and saw the repercussions, particularly for the person involved. I decided I never wanted to experience something like that again. I wanted to make sure we always do what we can to keep people safe.”

That motivation is now at the heart of Kenny's role at TDX.

"I'm really excited to have joined TDX. I'd heard some impressive things about the business before I arrived and, having spent the last couple of months visiting branches, I can see that there is already a strong commitment to safety.”

Kenny's focus will be on enhancing and improving the systems and processes already in place, with the support of the ELT.

"We've got some good systems in place, but there is always room to improve. My role is about making sure we're all on the same path and heading in the same direction.”

One of the first opportunities for everyone to have their say will be the upcoming TDX Safety Survey, which will run until the end of October.



Kenny is encouraging every TDX employee to take part, regardless of their role or location.

"The survey is really important because it gives everyone a voice. It's a bit like voting in the upcoming election, if you don't have your say, you can't contribute to the outcome.”

The survey isn't about pointing fingers or finding fault. Instead, it's about identifying gaps, understanding what's working and finding ways to make TDX even safer.

"Safety is a non-blame culture,” Kenny says.

"We're doing this survey so we can learn and help make things even better for everyone.”

Whether you work in a branch, warehouse, workshop, out in the field or in an office, Kenny's message is simple: take a few minutes, complete the survey and have your say.

"At the end of the day, we want everyone to go home safely to their loved ones. That's what matters most.”
